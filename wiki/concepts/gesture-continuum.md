# The gesture continuum

> **Different abstraction levels in gesture meaning ? increasing level of
> linguistic properties and semantics.**

```
Gesticulation ? [ Iconic ? Deictic ] ? Pantomimes ? Emblems ? Sign language
```

| Stage | Character (source) |
|---|---|
| **Gesticulation** | *spontaneous: accompany body expression* |
| **Iconic** | resembles what it denotes |
| **Deictic** | pointing |
| **Pantomimes** | ? |
| **Emblems** | conventional, e.g. **"Peace"** |
| **Sign language** | *communicative: supports or replaces speech* |

## What the axis actually orders

Two things move together along it:

1. **Dependence on speech falls.** Gesticulation cannot occur without speech;
   sign language does not need it.
2. **Conventionality rises.** At the left the form is improvised and readable
   from resemblance; at the right the form is arbitrary and must be **learned**.

That second point is why the lecture's caveat matters:

> **But: in different cultures, different meanings.**

An emblem is a **word**. It has no more natural connection to its meaning than a
spoken word does, which is precisely why it varies by culture ? and why
gesticulation does not.

> [!note] The continuum is a recognition-difficulty axis in disguise
> Left of the continuum, meaning is in the *motion* and recovering it is a
> spatiotemporal pattern problem. Right of it, meaning is in a *convention* and
> recognition is classification against a learned vocabulary. The lecture's
> summary says the model choice is **"dependent on gesture type ? gesture
> continuum"**, which is exactly this.

## Relation to L04

[[L04-embodied-language-processing]] argued that meaning is grounded in
sensorimotor experience. The gesture continuum is a **counter-gradient**: as you
move right, the sign becomes *less* iconic and *more* arbitrary, i.e. **less
grounded**, and works anyway.

Both things are true, and together they are more interesting than either: iconic
gestures show grounding working, emblems show a system that has left it behind.
The module presents them four lectures apart without connecting them.

## Deictic gestures and pointing

Deixis is singled out later: [[gwr-network]] is applied to **pointing in HRI**,
resolving which object is meant. Pointing is the gesture that **cannot** be
understood without the scene ? its content is a location, not a form ? and is
therefore the one case where recognition and [[multisensory-integration|fusion
with the visual scene]] cannot be separated. The link to
[[social-attention|gaze-following]] in L09 (the other *attend to what I am
attending to* mechanism) is also not drawn.

# Phrenology / Craniology

The **first systematic** attempt to relate brain and language — and a wrong one.

## What the notes say

- **Located language in anterior parts of the brain, at the production of the
  eye socket, below the eye.**
- **Bumps of the skull taken to reflect areas of enlargement in the brain.**

Contrasted directly with:

- **Empiricism: [[paul-broca]] — anatomical inspection of the brain.**

## Why it is in the lecture

Not as neuroscience but as **method**. The lecture's framing is
*phrenology vs empiricism*: one inferred brain structure from the outside of the
skull, the other opened the skull and looked.

The instructive part is that phrenology was **not entirely wrong** — it did put
language in the anterior brain, which is roughly where Broca's area turned out
to be. It got a right answer from an invalid method. Its two real errors were:

1. **The skull does not mirror the brain.** Cranial bumps do not track cortical
   volume.
2. **Faculties were assumed, not discovered.** The list of mental faculties was
   decided in advance and then mapped, instead of being derived from evidence.

The second error is the one with a modern afterlife: choosing your categories
first and then finding them in the data is still the standard way to get a
localisation result that does not replicate. `[external]`

## Connection to the rest of the wiki

Phrenology is the extreme of **local representation** — one faculty, one place.
The module's arc in L04 runs from here all the way to
[[embodied-language-representation]], where language *involves the whole brain*.
Compare [[local-vs-distributed-representation]], which L02 introduced at the
level of single neurons; this is the same axis at the level of whole faculties.

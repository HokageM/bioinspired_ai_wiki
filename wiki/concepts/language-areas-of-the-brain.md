# Language Areas of the Brain

The classical localisationist picture, presented in L04 as the *first systematic
studies of the relationship between brain and language* — and then superseded.

## The two areas

| Area | Function | Lobe | Aphasia on damage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Broca's area** ([[paul-broca]]) | **motor & production** | frontal | impaired language **production** |
| **Wernicke's area** ([[carl-wernicke]]) | **auditory & comprehension** | temporal (left superior) | impaired language **comprehension** |

- Broca's area demonstrates **localisation of functions in the cerebral cortex**.
- Wernicke's area — the **superior temporal area** is *where auditory words are
  stored*. There is a **connection between Wernicke and Broca**.

### Broca's aphasia, in detail (L04 p1)

- Impaired language production
- Sparse speech, non-fluent
- Deficits of intonation and stress patterns
- Lack of grammatical structure
- Omitting function words

Note what is *preserved*: comprehension. The patient knows what they want to say.

> [!note] A correction in the source
> On p2 the temporal-lobe entry was written as "impaired language production",
> struck through, and rewritten as "impaired language comprehension". The final
> version is correct.

## The two pathways

L04 recasts the areas as **functions**, i.e. as mappings:

- **Comprehension** — maps *"sound to meaning"*
  `speech → words → structures → meanings`
- **Production** — maps *"meaning to speech"*
  `meaning → grammatical encoding → phonological encoding → articulation`

These two chains are the target that [[multi-layer-associator]] is built to
implement — A1 (auditory form) at one end, M1 (articulatory form) at the other,
run forwards to produce and backwards to recognise.

## Why the model was rejected

**Early models rejected: anatomic and linguistic underspecification.**

- *Anatomic* — the areas have no sharp boundaries and the lesions that defined
  them were large and messy.
- *Linguistic* — "production" and "comprehension" are not primitive operations;
  each hides syntax, phonology, semantics.

**Imaging tools and new methods led to a major paradigm shift in models for
language processing** → [[dual-stream-hypothesis]] and
[[embodied-language-representation]].

## The historical arc

```
phrenology  →  Broca/Wernicke  →  dual stream  →  embodied, whole-brain
(guessing)     (localisation)     (pathways)      (distribution)
```

Each step widens the answer to *where is language?* — from one bump, to two
areas, to two streams, to the whole brain. See [[phrenology]].

# Dual-Stream Hypothesis

L04 names this as the example (`eg:`) of the modern view of language processing:
**dual stream hypothesis for processing, semantic maps.**

> [!info] Named, not developed
> The notes give the name and one line. Everything below the "What the notes
> say" section is either inference from adjacent material in the same lecture or
> explicitly marked `[external]`.

## What the notes say

From the *Human language processing: Functions* section (p2):

- **Comprehension**: maps *"sound to meaning"* — labelled **dorsal**
  `speech → words`, `structures → meanings`
- **Production**: maps *"meaning to speech"* — labelled **ventral**
  `meaning → grammatical encoding`, `phonological encoding → articulation`

A margin sketch shows a brain with **dorsal** arrow arcing over the top and
**ventral** below.

And from p3:
- Language processing involves **several pathways**, **re-uses parts of the
  brain in both production and comprehension**, and shows **spatial and temporal
  hierarchical abstraction**.

> [!warning] The labels are swapped — **confirmed at L06**
> In the standard dual-stream account the **dorsal** stream is the
> sound-to-**articulation** route (sensorimotor, production) and the **ventral**
> stream is the sound-to-**meaning** route (comprehension). `[external]`
> The notes assign them the other way round. A mnemonic: ventral is the "what"
> stream in vision too, and *what* is meaning.
>
> **This is no longer an external correction.**
> [[L06-hierarchical-vision]] p4 states outright: **Dorsal = Visual Spatial
> Location (Where?), Ventral = Object Recognition (What?)** — and p5 confirms
> it with the projection targets (ventral → temporal cortex, dorsal → parietal
> cortex) and cites lesion studies. See [[two-visual-streams]].
>
> So **the module contradicts itself**, and the later lecture is the correct
> one. The text above is kept as L04 wrote it; the error is recorded, not
> silently fixed.

## Relation to the visual streams

The ventral/dorsal *what/where* distinction is originally a finding about
**vision**, attributed on [[two-visual-streams]] to [[leslie-ungerleider]]. The
language version described on this page is an **adaptation** of it to the
auditory domain, not an independent discovery — the anatomy is the same, since
ventral runs forward along the temporal lobe and dorsal up into the parietal
lobe in both cases.

That relationship is what makes L04's swap detectable at all: if *ventral =
what* in vision, then mapping *sound → meaning* (a "what" operation) onto the
dorsal stream cannot be right.

| | Vision (L06) | Language (L04, as written) | Language (corrected) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ventral** | what — object recognition | production | comprehension, sound → meaning |
| **Dorsal** | where — spatial location | comprehension | production, sound → articulation |


## What it replaces

The classical [[language-areas-of-the-brain]] model: two boxes joined by one
cable. The dual-stream view replaces boxes with **routes** — the same cortical
territory participates in both, so "where is production" stops being a
well-formed question.

This is the hinge of the lecture's argument. Once language is a set of
overlapping pathways rather than a set of regions, the step to
[[embodied-language-representation]] — *involves the whole brain* — is short.

# Leslie Ungerleider

Named in [[L06-hierarchical-vision]] p5 as the discoverer of the ventral/dorsal
partition of visual processing.

## What the source says

One clause:

> **Partitioning found by Ungerleider.**

Immediately followed by: *separation underpinned by **lesion studies***.

The notes give **no first name**, no date, no co-author, no publication. The
first name used as this page's title is `[external]`, as is the standard
co-attribution to **Mortimer Mishkin**, who is not mentioned in the notes at
all. The 1982 ventral/dorsal partition is conventionally credited to
Ungerleider *and* Mishkin jointly.

> [!note] Recorded as a gap, not corrected
> The wiki does not silently add Mishkin to the lecture's claim. The attribution
> above is flagged as external so the source's own omission stays visible.

## The finding

See [[two-visual-streams]] for the substance:

| Stream | Projects to | Encodes | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ventral** | temporal cortex | shape, colour, texture | **what?** |
| **Dorsal** | parietal cortex | spatial information | **where?** |

The evidential basis given is **lesion studies** — damage one stream and one
capacity fails while the other survives. That is a *double dissociation*, which
is what makes the partition a claim about separate mechanisms rather than a
correlation. The notes cite the method without naming a single study.

## Why it matters in this wiki

This finding is what resolves an internal contradiction in the module: L06's
*dorsal = where, ventral = what* contradicts
[[L04-embodied-language-processing]], which labelled comprehension as dorsal.
See [[dual-stream-hypothesis]]. Ungerleider's visual partition is the original;
the language dual-stream model is an adaptation of it.

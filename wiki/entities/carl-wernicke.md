# Carl Wernicke

Named in L04 as the discoverer of the brain's comprehension area.

> [!info] Named, not described
> The notes give the name and the finding, nothing else. No dates, no context.

## What the notes say

- **Carl Wernicke: (auditory & comprehension)**
- Area of the **left superior temporal lobe**
- **Superior temporal area: where auditory words are stored**
- **Connection between Wernicke and Broca**
- **Wernicke's aphasia: impaired language comprehension**

## Why he matters to the lecture's argument

Broca alone gives you one area and one function. Wernicke gives you **two areas,
two functions, and a cable between them** — which is the first actual *model* of
language in the brain rather than a single localisation. The two-box-plus-cable
picture is what the [[multi-layer-associator]] later implements as A1 ⇄ M1.

The contrast between the two aphasias is also what makes the model testable:
Broca's patients understand but cannot speak, Wernicke's speak fluently but
without comprehension. Two dissociable deficits imply two dissociable systems.

> [!note] The lecture then rejects this
> *Early models rejected: anatomic and linguistic underspecification.*
> See [[dual-stream-hypothesis]] and [[language-areas-of-the-brain]].

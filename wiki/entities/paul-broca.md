# Paul Broca

French physician, cited in L04 as the **empiricist** counterpoint to
[[phrenology]].

> [!info] Named, not described
> The lecture gives no dates, no biography and no first name — the notes say
> only "Broca". Everything here beyond the two bullets below is either
> inferred from the lecture's own context or marked `[external]`.

## What the notes say

- **Empiricism: Broca — anatomical inspection of the brain.**
- **Broca's area: motor & production.**
  - Localisation of functions in the cerebral cortex
  - **Broca's aphasia**: impaired language production, sparse and non-fluent
    speech, deficits of intonation and stress patterns, lack of grammatical
    structure, omitting function words

## Why he is in the lecture

Broca is the lecture's demonstration that **method matters**. Phrenology
inferred function from the outside of the skull and got a plausible answer for
bad reasons; Broca inferred it from lesion plus autopsy and got a defensible
one. The lecture's own word is *empiricism*.

His result also founded **localisation of function** — the idea that a specific
cognitive capacity can be assigned to a specific piece of cortex. L04 then
spends the rest of its pages dismantling that idea in favour of
[[embodied-language-representation]]. Broca is both the start of the modern
story and the position the lecture ends up rejecting.

`[external]` Paul Pierre Broca, 1824–1880. The 1861 patient known as "Tan" —
after the only syllable he could produce — is the canonical case.

# Lloyd Jeffress

Named in L05 only as **"Early Jeffress neuronal coincidence model"**.

> [!info] Named, not described
> No first name, no dates, no citation. The notes use the surname as a label for
> the model. Everything below the first section is `[external]`.

## What the notes attribute to him

A model in which axons from the two ears act as **delay lines** running towards
each other, with **coincidence detector** neurons along them, so that the
position of the firing detector encodes the
[[interaural-time-difference]]. See [[jeffress-model]].

## `[external]`

Lloyd A. Jeffress (1900–1986), American psychologist. He proposed the
place-theory of sound localisation in 1948 — remarkably, as a purely
theoretical construction, before any of the anatomy was known. Delay lines and
coincidence detectors were subsequently found in the barn owl's nucleus
laminaris, a striking confirmation.

The mammalian case is less settled; current evidence suggests the MSO may encode
ITD through inhibitory timing and a population rate code rather than a literal
delay-line array.

## Why he is worth a page

The Jeffress model is the module's best example of the bio-inspired programme
working *in reverse*. Usually the story is: observe biology, then build. Here a
computational argument — *how could a nervous system possibly measure
microseconds?* — produced a mechanism that was later found in real brains.

It is also the one place where a biological mechanism and a standard algorithm
turn out to be the same computation; see [[cross-correlation-localisation]] and
[[ann-brain-correspondence]].
